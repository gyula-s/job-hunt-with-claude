# Career interview questions

Ask these one at a time, roughly in this order, for each role. Choose the next question based on the last answer and rephrase it to fit the person's field. Skip anything already answered by their documents, but confirm what the documents said.

## Each role

1. Job title and start and end dates (month and year). Was the title ever different from what you actually did?
2. In a sentence or two, what does the organisation do, and how big is it?
3. How big was your team, who did you report to, and where did you fit?
4. What did a normal week look like?
5. What tools, systems, methods or equipment did you use day to day?
6. Which piece of work are you most proud of? Tell me what you did yourself.
7. What was the hardest problem you dealt with, and what did you do about it?
8. Did anything get measurably better because of you: time, money, quality, errors, customers, sales? A rough figure is fine.
9. What did you own on your own, and what was a team effort?
10. Did you lead, train, mentor, review or manage anyone or anything?
11. Did you win anything, get promoted, or get praised for something specific?
12. How did it end up? Did things land on time, and what went wrong?
13. Why did you leave, or why are you looking now? (Only for forms; they can decline.)
14. Is there anything about this role you'd rather not mention or emphasise?

When they say "I don't remember", suggest where to look (old emails, repos, tickets, appraisals, payslips, LinkedIn) and move on. Never fill a gap with a guess.

## Across the career

- Qualifications: institution, subject, dates, grade or classification. Check against a certificate or transcript where possible.
- Certifications and training, with dates.
- Languages, and how well they speak each.
- Volunteering, side projects, publications, talks, open-source work.
- Interests worth one line on a CV.
- Gaps in the timeline: what they did, and how they'd like it described.
- What they want more of and less of in their next job (useful for "why are you leaving" and "why us").
- Stories for interviews: a conflict, a failure, a tight deadline, a decision with little information. Collect one of each in the notes.
